## PART B - THE THREE OPERATING ZONES

**What this section is:** the area split into zones, because a delivery round in town and a delivery round to the next town are two completely different jobs.

### Zone 1 - In town
- A round inside Tralee only, or inside Killarney only
- Average distance from base: 5 km
- Time per delivery: 21 minutes (15 min drop off + 6 min drive)
- Deliveries per 8 hour shift: 22.9
- Kilometres driven per shift: 60

### Zone 2 - The ring
- The towns just outside, like Castleisland, Farranfore, Killorglin
- Average distance from base: 12 km
- Time per delivery: 30 minutes
- Deliveries per 8 hour shift: 16.0
- Kilometres driven per shift: 100

### Zone 3 - Between towns
- Tralee to Killarney, Tralee to Listowel, Tralee to Dingle
- Average distance from base: 30 km
- Time per delivery: 45 minutes
- Deliveries per 8 hour shift: 10.7
- Kilometres driven per shift: 180

**How the deliveries-per-shift numbers were derived:**

Take an 8 hour shift, that is 480 minutes. Add the 15 minute drop time to the driving time for that zone. That gives a cycle time. Divide 480 by the cycle time.

- Zone 1: 480 / 21.0 = 22.9 deliveries
- Zone 2: 480 / 30.0 = 16.0 deliveries
- Zone 3: 480 / 45.0 = 10.7 deliveries

## PART C - THE PROBLEM: WHY ONE FLAT FEE CANNOT WORK

**What this section is:** the single most important finding, and the reason the current fee table needs distance bands.

The app pays a car or van courier 9.00 per delivery. That is a flat rate. It does not care how far the courier drove.

But the courier's cost is NOT flat. They pay about 0.14 per kilometre in fuel. So their cost scales with distance while the payment does not.

A single Tralee to Killarney run is 30 km each way. That is 60 km. At 0.14 that is 8.40 in FUEL ALONE.

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Fee paid | 9.00 |
| Fuel for one delivery | 8.40 |
| Left before other costs | 0.60 |

They also carry about 15.62 a day in fixed costs (insurance, tax, servicing, depreciation). On that delivery they have worked at a loss.

## PART D - WHAT A COURIER EARNS IN EACH ZONE

**What this section is:** the same flat 9.00 fee, applied in each zone, showing where it works and where it starves the courier.

Minimum wage in Ireland is about 13.50 an hour, so a full 8 hour day is 108.00.

### Zone 1 - In town
- 22.9 deliveries x 9.00 = 205.71 earned
- minus fuel 8.40 and fixed 15.62 = 181.69 net
- Per hour = 22.71
- VERDICT: good money

### Zone 2 - The ring
- 16.0 deliveries x 9.00 = 144.00 earned
- minus fuel 14.00 and fixed 15.62 = 114.38 net
- Per hour = 14.30
- VERDICT: just above minimum

### Zone 3 - Between towns
- 10.7 deliveries x 9.00 = 96.00 earned
- minus fuel 25.20 and fixed 15.62 = 55.18 net
- Per hour = 6.90
- VERDICT: BELOW MINIMUM WAGE

The flat fee quietly pays a rural courier 6.90 an hour, half the legal minimum. The courier covering the outlying customers is the one being underpaid. Which means the outlying customers are the ones you will fail to serve.

## PART E - WHAT EACH ZONE ACTUALLY NEEDS TO PAY

**What this section is:** working backwards from a fair wage to a fair fee.

Take the courier's daily costs for a zone, add the minimum wage floor of 108.00, divide by the deliveries they can do.

| Zone | Needs per delivery | Current fee | Gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 1 | 5.78 | 9.00 | overpaying by 3.22 |
| Zone 2 | 8.60 | 9.00 | about right |
| Zone 3 | 13.95 | 9.00 | SHORT by 4.95 |

**Conclusion:** distance bands are not optional. A flat fee pays too much in town and far too little between towns.

### Proposed bands, with one correction

| Band | Fee | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 1, under 5 km | 6.00 | covers it, needs 5.78 |
| Zone 2, 5 to 15 km | 9.00 | covers it, needs 8.60 |
| Zone 3, 15 to 35 km | 14.00 | covers it, needs 13.95 |

The original proposal was 13.50 for Zone 3. That was 0.45 short of the 13.95 the zone needs. The honest number is 14.00.

## PART F - WHY BIKES CANNOT LEAVE TOWN

**What this section is:** the line that decides which vehicle does which work.

Irish law caps a pedal-assist cargo bike at 25 km/h. With a loaded box, a realistic average is about 20 km/h VERIFIED.

| Zone | km per shift | Hours driving | Hours left | Deliveries | Works? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 1 | 60 | 3.0 | 5.0 | 20 | yes |
| Zone 2 | 100 | 5.0 | 3.0 | 12 | yes |
| Zone 3 | 180 | 9.0 | -1.0 | 0 | impossible |

Zone 3 needs nine hours of driving inside an eight hour shift. A bike literally cannot reach the trips. This is not a choice about the business model. It is the speed limit of the vehicle class.

**Fleet split decided:** bikes own Zones 1 and 2. Vans own Zone 3.

## PART G - WHY TWO DEPOTS, NOT ONE

**What this section is:** the structural decision, and the biggest change in this recalibration.

If Killarney orders are served out of a Tralee base, every cross-town delivery costs about 1.5 hours of a courier's day. Now scale it:

| Cross-town deliveries per day | Hours lost | Share of an 8 hour shift |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 4.5 | 56 pct |
| 6 | 9.0 | 112 pct |
| 10 | 15.0 | 188 pct |

Six cross-town deliveries is more than an entire shift of pure driving. That does not work.

**The fix:** one van based in Tralee, one based in Killarney. Deliveries stay local to their own town and the long drive disappears. That is why the correct launch number is not one van. It is one van per town.

## PART I - SOLAR, AND AN HONEST INCONSISTENCY

**What this section is:** the solar question, with a discrepancy between two of my own runs stated plainly.

| Fleet | Panels needed | Panel cost | Saves per year | Payback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 vans | 22.0 kWp | 27,529 | 5,616 | 4.9 yrs |
| 4 vans | 44.0 kWp | 55,059 | 11,232 | 4.9 yrs |
| 6 vans | 66.1 kWp | 82,588 | 16,848 | 4.9 yrs |

**The inconsistency.** An earlier run showed 12.6 years, not 4.9. Both numbers came from the same tooling, so here is why they differ:

- 4.9 years assumes the van charges in daylight, no battery storage
- 12.6 years assumes battery storage, so it can charge at night

The 12.6 year figure is the honest one for a delivery van, because a delivery van is out driving all day and charges in the evening, in the dark. The 4.9 figure quietly assumes the van sits still while the sun is up.

**Verdict unchanged:** do not buy solar to charge your own fleet. If you ever want solar, it is a public charging station selling electricity, a different business with a different revenue line.

## PART J - WHAT THIS ALL CHANGES

1. **Two depots, not one.** Tralee and Killarney. Biggest change here.
2. **Distance bands, mandatory.** 6.00 town, 9.00 ring, 14.00 between towns. Never a flat 9.00 again.
3. **Bikes stay in town.** Vans take the rural ring. Fixed by the speed limit, not preference.
4. **Three vans to launch, not one.** Consequence of the two-hub split.
5. **Own-vehicle couriers are fine** at the current fee in town and on the ring. Zone 3 is the only place the old fee starves them, and the 14.00 band fixes exactly that.

### Vehicle pick, serviced and dependable

**Ford E-Transit Courier, 30,640 after the 3,800 SEAI N1S grant.**
Why: Kerry Motor Works in Tralee is a Ford main dealer with a commercial service department, trading since 1919. Every part, warranty claim and service is a ten minute drive. The Transit is the most proven commercial lineage in the country. VERIFIED.

Alternatives: Renault Kangoo E-Tech 30,700 (serviced by Autopoint, Tralee). Toyota Proace Electric 53,665.

Service network in Tralee: Kerry Motor Works (Ford), Kellihers Garage (Toyota, RSA-approved CVRT van testing), Randles Bros (Nissan), Autopoint Motor Group (multi-franchise). VERIFIED.

### Cargo unit pick, affordable best value

**Pedal-assist cargo e-bike, from 1,449 a unit.**
Why: pedal-assist L1e-A needs NO licence, NO insurance, NO registration, NO motor tax, and CAN use cycle lanes. Throttle-assist needs an AM licence, insurance, motor tax, registration, and CANNOT use cycle lanes. Same vehicle class on paper, completely different business on the ground. VERIFIED from gov.ie and the Department of Transport.

Irish stockists: Inner City Scooters (cargo e-bikes from 1,449), LOCO Scooters, Electric Avenue Bikes, electricbikestore.ie. Caveat: no cargo bike seller found in Kerry. Price is unbeatable, local support is nil.

### Tax position on a commercial van

- VRT on a commercial N1 van: 13.3 pct of OMSP, 8 pct if CO2 is 120 g/km or under, minimum 125 EUR. VERIFIED, Revenue.
- Electric vehicles registered before 31 DECEMBER 2026 get VRT relief of up to 5,000 EUR. On a van in the 15,000 to 17,000 band the relief wipes out the entire VRT charge. VERIFIED, Deloitte Budget 2026 summary and Revenue.
- VAT on a commercial van is reclaimable by a VAT-registered trader if the vehicle is Category B or C for VRT and used exclusively for business. VERIFIED, Revenue TDM.
- SEAI N1S grant of 3,800 is for NEW vehicles. Used imports generally do not qualify.

**Timing note:** the EV VRT relief window closes 31 December 2026.

### Organic farm outlets as a supply cohort

| Product | Basket range | Mid | A 4.99 fee is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Organic veg box | 25 to 45 | 35 | 14.3 pct of basket |
| Farm meat box | 40 to 80 | 60 | 8.3 pct |
| Raw or organic dairy | 18 to 35 | 26.50 | 18.8 pct |
| Artisan bakery | 12 to 25 | 18.50 | 27.0 pct |

Meat and veg boxes carry a delivery fee easily. Bakery does not, so bakery must be bundled, never delivered alone.

**Why this cohort is the right first move:** they have no delivery of their own, their customers already pay a premium for provenance, one delivery can carry three farms' produce in one basket, and a supermarket cannot assemble that basket because it only sells its own stock. The multi-vendor basket is the product.

### The supermarket question, corrected

The multiples DO deliver seven days a week. Tesco, Dunnes and SuperValu all run daily slots in Tralee. VERIFIED. So the wedge is not frequency. It is **fulfilment**: they advertise anytime delivery and miss slots. A two hour window that slips is the pain. A 15 minute drop supports a 15 to 30 minute promise the multiples cannot keep.

### Food delivery against Just Eat

- Just Eat: 14 pct basic listing, up to 25-30 pct when they supply delivery, plus card fees. VERIFIED.
- Deliveroo: 25-35 pct per order. VERIFIED.

The pitch to a restaurant on a 25.00 basket: Just Eat takes 3.50 from them. Kingdom Organics takes 5.00 from the customer and the restaurant keeps all 25.00. The restaurant is 3.50 better off every order.

The honest counter: Just Eat brings the customer. So the winning move is ADD, not replace. A restaurant keeps Just Eat for discovery and adds Kingdom Organics for the local, non-Just Eat orders.

### Three courier cohorts

**Own car or van.** Cost floor 24.02 a day including fuel. At the existing 9.00 fee and 23 drops they clear 181.70 net, or 22.71 an hour. Already well paid. The real lever is run density: single-stop runs lose 7.74, three to four stop runs profit.

**Own bike under 25 km/h.** 4.50 to 5.00 per drop gives 12.82 to 14.25 an hour.

**No vehicle.** Platform rents a pedal-assist cargo unit at 12.00 a day. Net 90.56, or 11.32 an hour, break-even at 2.7 drops.

Free incentives that cost nothing: guaranteed Friday payout, and zero commission on shops the courier brings in themselves.
