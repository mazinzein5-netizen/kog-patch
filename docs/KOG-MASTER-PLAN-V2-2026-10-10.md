## 1. MISSION

Distance-based grocery and marketplace delivery for independent Kerry grocers: organic, fresh meat and poultry, halal. Asset-light, courier-first, capital-light. Keeps local spend local.

## 2. MARKET (kept from the PDF, it is correct)

7 towns, 54,279 combined population.
- Tralee 26,079 (48.0%) - Primary hub, Depot 1
- Killarney 14,500 (26.7%) - Second hub, Depot 2
- Listowel 4,800, Castleisland 2,500, Kenmare 2,400, Killorglin 2,200, Dingle 1,800
Two-hub core = 40,579 = 74.7% of addressable market. Pilot households ~15,600.
Scale target: 22 stores.

## 3. THE TWO-STAGE PILOT (founder decision)

STAGE A - TRALEE ONLY. Single hub. Reason: order density is the real constraint, not price. Splitting a thin pilot order book across two towns halves courier pay and churns drivers. Own Tralee first.
STAGE B - add Killarney once Stage A holds 6+ drops/day steady for 4 weeks.

Pilot couriers use their OWN cars (Cohort C). No vans, no e-bikes, no vehicle purchase. Zero fleet capital. Vans and e-bikes are Phase 3 only (see section 7).

## 4. STANDING ECONOMICS (the truth model - this replaces the old 5% flat)

Average basket EUR 50. Card processing EUR 0.55 per transaction.

Zone A (up to 5 km): store commission 15% = 7.50 + customer delivery fee 3.99 = 11.49 revenue; driver pay 6.50; NET 4.44
Zone B (5-12 km): commission 20% = 10.00 + fee 5.49 = 15.49 revenue; driver pay 10.00; NET 4.94
Zone C (12+ km): commission 28% = 14.00 + fee 6.99 = 20.99 revenue; driver pay 15.50; NET 4.94

HONEST PITCH LINE (replaces the contradictory 5% / keep-100% claim):
"We take 15% on a local order. Just Eat takes 25 to 35%. You keep your customer data and your prices."
Effective platform take rate is ~10% of a EUR 50 basket. That genuinely undercuts aggregators. Say it plainly; do not promise 5%.

Pilot founding rate: for the first 3 signed stores, a founding commission of 8% (not 3%) for 12 months. Reason: 3% on Zone A nets the platform a LOSS of 1.56 per drop. 8% keeps the founding offer generous AND solvent. If the founder insists on 3%, the LEADER A&D grant must underwrite the gap - that is what that grant funds.

## 5. COURIER PAY - PER TRIP WITH A FLOOR (founder decision)

Drop = one customer order. Trip = a batched run of 3-4 drops from one hub.
Pay per TRIP, not raw per drop, with a minimum-shift floor so thin pilot days do not churn drivers.

- Minimum shift guarantee: EUR 40 for a 3-hour booked window (about EUR 13.33/hr, at the min-wage floor).
- Above the floor, the courier earns the per-drop rate (6.50 / 10.00 / 15.50) for every drop completed.
- Courier keeps 100% of the driver fee the platform pays them. Nothing is deducted from their pay. (The customer delivery fee belongs to the platform - it funds dispatch, insurance and support. Do NOT tell couriers they keep the delivery fee; that was the old error.)
- Own-car couriers are reimbursed nothing extra; the per-drop rate is set to cover fuel. Distance bands protect them on long runs.

## 6. VAT CLIFF - MANAGED, NOT FEARED (founder decision)

Irish services registration threshold is about EUR 37,500 turnover (trailing 12 months).

Key insight: the threshold counts TOTAL turnover, but the true margin hit is small, because:
- Store commissions are B2B. Stores are VAT-registered and RECLAIM the VAT you charge them. So commission revenue is VAT-neutral to the store.
- Only the consumer-facing delivery fees carry unrecoverable VAT. That is the thin slice.

Stage A Tralee-only turnover: ~450 drops over 12 weeks x ~11.49 = about EUR 5,200, annualised ~EUR 22,000. UNDER the threshold. Stage A is comfortably VAT-free.
Stage B / scaling toward 22 stores crosses EUR 37,500. Plan for it:
1. Register voluntarily just before the crossing; do not get caught.
2. Once registered, reclaim input VAT on insurance, lease, charging, equipment - that offsets most of the output VAT on B2B commission.
3. The real net cost is VAT on consumer delivery fees only. Either absorb it (margin drops ~1 on Zone A) or bake it into the zone fee table. Do NOT surcharge at checkout - drip fees kill conversion and trust.
4. Keep the consumer delivery fee low (3.99) as a deliberate choice: it is the only VAT-exposed line, so a low fee means a low cliff.

## 7. FLEET - PHASE 3 ONLY, CORRECT RENTS (founder decision: no cash for vans now)

Pilot: zero vehicles. Own-car couriers.
Phase 3 (grant-funded, months 3-6): introduce vehicles. NEVER quote 13 as a rent - that is the LEASE COST, not a price. Verified all-in cost per day, insurance included:

- Cargo e-bike: all-in 5.32/day (bought at 1,449). Rent 9/day. Margin 3.68/day.
- Electric moped: rent 20/day. AM licence.
- Electric van: all-in 35.01/day leased (lease 13.16 + charging 8.00 + insurance 9.62 + servicing 2.31 + damage 1.92); 33.35/day if owned (BYD ETP3 14,950 over 5 yr). Rent 35/day. At 13/day you lose about 22/day per van.

Launch fleet when funded: 3 electric vans + 4-6 cargo e-bikes.

## 8. QUIET MARGIN LEVERS (legal, no dark patterns) - founder asked for these

These lift platform profit WITHOUT a visible price rise, so they do not feel like a fee hike to stores or customers:

1. Bigger baskets. Push EUR 60-80 organic boxes and halal meat boxes, not single-item orders. Commission is a % of basket, so a 70 basket earns 10.50 vs 7.50 on a 50 basket, same 15% rate, no price change anyone notices.
2. Early-payout fee for stores. Settle a store instantly for 1% instead of weekly. Voluntary, transparent, pure margin.
3. Store-paid featured placement. A store can pay to sit at the top of its category, clearly labelled as promoted (Amazon-style). Honest auction of attention you already own.
4. Consolidated-slot discount. Offer customers a lower fee if they pick a shared delivery window. This raises drops-per-trip, which is where the real cost saving is.
5. Own-brand staple. A Kingdom Organics branded staple box (eggs, oats, rice) sourced direct, sold on-platform at a small own-margin.
6. Peak windows. A transparent higher delivery fee for the Friday 7-9pm slot, shown before checkout, never added after.
7. Aggregated demand data back to suppliers. Anonymised, GDPR-safe category trend reports sold to Kerry producers. (Only with clear consent and no personal data.)

WHAT WE WILL NOT DO (this is the moat, do not poison it):
- No drip pricing or fees added at the last step.
- No hidden auto-renewals on stores.
- No bait-and-switch commission that jumps after onboarding.
- No taking the courier's delivery fee while telling them they keep 100%.
Store owners ARE the product. One dark pattern and the whole "we are the honest local alternative" pitch is dead.

## 10. FUNDING (kept, all verified)

- Kerry LEO Feasibility Grant: 50% up to EUR 15,000, no matching funds required.

Framing for funders: this is NOT "fund us or we die." The pilot self-funds at ~6 drops/day with zero capital. The grant buys SPEED and the research that turns 3 stores into 22. A project that cannot die if they say no is the strongest card you have.
